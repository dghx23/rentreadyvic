04

What you are required to give — and what you are not

Victoria now uses a prescribed rental application form. RentReady separates information that can be requested from information that cannot be requested, and explains discrimination in practical terms.

Prescribed application form. From 31 March 2026, rental providers and agents must use the prescribed rental application form and can only ask for information set out in that form. Consumer Affairs Victoria ↗

They may ask you for

Personal details

Full name, date of birth, current address, phone number and email.

Employment details

Current or most recent employer, employer address, net weekly income and, if not currently employed, when the most recent employment ended.

Financial evidence

No more than two documents from the prescribed list to show capacity to pay rent, such as current/recent payslips, a bank statement without transaction details, or a Centrelink payment statement or letter.

Identity documents

No more than two identity documents from the prescribed list, such as a driver licence, passport, Medicare card, Centrelink card or another listed document.

Rental or residential history

Previous addresses and rental-provider contact details may be requested.

Personal referees

If requested, you may provide the referee's name, phone number, email and connection to you.

Preferred lease and move-in date

You can advise your desired rental term and preferred move-in date.

They cannot ask you for

Disputes or legal action with a previous rental provider

Your application cannot ask whether you have taken legal action or had a dispute with a rental provider.

Bond history

They cannot ask about your bond history, including whether a claim has been made on a previous bond.

Daily bank transactions

If a bank statement is requested, daily transaction details are not required; private transactions can be removed.

Questions outside the prescribed form

They cannot add extra application questions that are not part of the prescribed rental application form.

Protected attributes without a written reason

If they ask about a protected attribute, they must provide the reason for asking in writing.

What discrimination means here

Official guidance ↗

A rental provider can choose between applicants using lawful factors such as rental history and ability to pay. What they cannot do is treat you unfavourably because of a protected personal characteristic. Consumer Affairs Victoria gives examples including age, disability, race and religion.

If a provider or agent asks about a protected characteristic, they must tell you in writing why that information is required. The application must also include information about unlawful discrimination.

Having children aged 16 or under is generally not a lawful reason to refuse a renter, subject to limited exceptions concerning the provider's main residence or a property genuinely unsuitable for children.

References

If a personal referee is requested, the prescribed application allows contact details including the referee's name, phone, email and connection to you. The earlier RentReady reference-request workflow is preserved in the project archive while this new income-first flow is being integrated.
