Prepare to apply

Keep the application strong and focused.

More personal information is not automatically a stronger application. Start with the permitted evidence that directly supports identity, rental history and capacity to pay.

1Choose your financial evidence

Use current evidence that shows capacity to pay. A Centrelink payment statement or letter can be relevant where income support forms part of your income.

2Choose identity evidence

Provide documents from the prescribed options rather than sending extra identity material “just in case”.

3Prepare rental history and referees

Have previous-address information and permitted referee contact details ready where requested.

4Protect bank privacy

If a bank statement is used, detailed day-to-day transactions do not need to become an open-ended view of your spending.

5Keep a copy of the application

Save the listing, form, screenshots, emails and messages in case you later need to show exactly what was requested.

6Keep RentReady and ARights separate

Your affordability result can help you decide whether to apply; it does not change what an agent is lawfully permitted to request.

Victorian application rules

Click a rule to understand it.

These explainers keep the legal/application layer separate from RentReady's financial calculations.

Prescribed application form
Why it matters: Victoria restricts the information that can be requested in a rental application. Extra questions should not be treated as automatically acceptable just because an agent or platform displays them.
Consumer Affairs Victoria ↗
Financial evidence
Why it matters: Financial evidence is for assessing capacity to pay, not for unrestricted access to private financial behaviour. Use the permitted evidence that proves the point without oversharing.
Identity evidence
Why it matters: Identity checking should remain tied to the prescribed application process. Sending additional identity documents can increase privacy and security exposure without strengthening the application.
Bank statements
Why it matters: A bank statement can be financial evidence, but that does not make detailed day-to-day transactions an unrestricted application requirement. Keep unrelated transaction information private where permitted.
Tenancy-database checks
Why it matters: If a tenancy database is being used, the applicant should be told which database is being checked. Keep a record of what you were told.
Protected personal characteristics
Why it matters: Questions or treatment connected with a protected characteristic may engage Victorian discrimination law. A request for protected-characteristic information should not be treated as routine without understanding why it is being sought.
Consumer Affairs Victoria ↗

Common application information that may be requested

Personal and contact details

Name, contact details and other information contained in the prescribed application process.

Employment and income information

Information relevant to capacity to pay rent, including permitted evidence of employment or other income.

Rental or residential history

Previous addresses and rental-provider contact information may be relevant to rental history.

Personal referees

Permitted referee details can include name, contact details and their connection to you.

Preferred lease term and move-in date

The application can include the rental term sought and the date you would like the tenancy to begin.

Requests that should make you stop and check

Questions about previous bond claims

Bond-history questions are not a general invitation to investigate your past bond disputes or claims.

Questions about prior rental disputes or legal action

These are distinct from ordinary rental-history information and should be checked against the prescribed application rules.

Detailed daily banking transactions

Financial evidence should not become an unrestricted review of every transaction you make.

Extra platform questions

An optional-looking field on a third-party application platform is not automatically permitted merely because the platform asks it.

Protected-characteristic questions without explanation

Ask why the information is required and keep the explanation in writing.

Fair treatment

Discrimination is different from ordinary applicant selection.

A rental provider may assess legitimate matters such as rental history and capacity to pay. They cannot lawfully treat an applicant unfavourably because of a protected personal characteristic, subject to applicable exceptions.

Income support and Centrelink
Being on an income-support payment is not automatically the same thing as a protected characteristic. Capacity to pay can be assessed. But the reason a person receives a payment may overlap with a protected characteristic such as disability, age or parent/carer status.
Disability and assistance dogs
Disability is a protected characteristic. Treatment connected with disability or an assistance dog can raise discrimination issues. Keep written evidence if this affects the application.
Children and parent/carer status
Parent or carer status can be protected. If family status appears to change how an application is processed, record exactly what happened and check the applicable guidance.
Age
Age is protected. A provider may assess whether rent can be paid but should not substitute age itself for a legitimate assessment criterion.
Victimisation
Treating a person badly because they made or supported a discrimination complaint can itself raise a separate concern.
Need help?
Consumer Affairs Victoria deals with rental-law compliance. The Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission provides discrimination information and dispute-resolution pathways.

If something looks wrong

Keep evidence, ask for the rule, and use the right pathway.

1Ask what authorises the request

Ask the agent to identify why the information is required and how it fits the Victorian application rules.

2Get the reason in writing

Written explanations are easier to assess and preserve if a question or decision later needs review.

3Keep evidence

Save screenshots, forms, listings, emails, messages, receipts and any written explanation.

4Use Consumer Affairs Victoria

Use the official rental-law guidance and complaint pathway for Victorian tenancy/application issues.

5Use VEOHRC for discrimination

If the concern is treatment connected to a protected characteristic, review the Victorian discrimination guidance.

6Return to RentReady for affordability

Keep legal/application questions separate from whether a property fits your income and rent position.
